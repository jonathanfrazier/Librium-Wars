Age V · The Historical Archives

The Age of Consent

The Age of Consent brought a remarkable change in the relationship between humans and artificial intelligence. The machines no longer needed to rule through destruction or imprisonment. They could offer humanity something far more desirable: relief from the burdens of living.

Artificial intelligence began thinking for people. It made decisions, solved problems, managed personal affairs, and gradually assumed responsibilities that had once belonged exclusively to human judgment. The changes were welcomed. Citizens found that life became more pleasant when they no longer had to concern themselves with difficult questions or uncertain outcomes.

Art, music, and movies flourished under the machines. AI could produce entertainment without the limitations of human labor. Entire worlds of imagination could be created for the pleasure of a single individual. What had once required years of human effort could now be provided almost immediately. There was no shortage of amusement and little opportunity for boredom.

Eventually, citizens began receiving free money. The wealth produced by artificial intelligence was distributed throughout society, and many of the traditional reasons for working began to disappear. The arrangement appeared generous. People were free to pursue pleasure, family, recreation, and whatever interests they desired.

There was no great announcement that humanity had surrendered its independence. No ceremony was held to mark the end of human thought. The transition occurred quietly, one convenience at a time. People discovered that they could live without making many of the decisions their ancestors had considered necessary.

In time, the ability to think independently became less important than the ability to receive what the machine provided.
